## LinkedIn

A $3,450 mortgage next to a $2,300 rent check looks like an easy call. Then you run the full first-year numbers.

In our Lee's Summit example, a $400,000 home with 10% down had a net cost of about $28,200 in year one, after 3% appreciation and the return lost on the cash. Renting came to about $27,800. Nearly identical.

So the decision shifts to cash flow. The buyer ties up $52,000 in down payment and closing costs. If that comes out of the business at a roughly 30% combined marginal rate, it takes about $74,000 of pre-tax profit to net it. That same money could pay down a 9% line of credit, fund a retirement plan, or cover a slow-paying month of payroll.

Lenders add a wrinkle for self-employed owners. They qualify you on net income after deductions, so a Schedule C showing $62,000 on $480,000 of revenue supports a much smaller loan than most owners expect.
