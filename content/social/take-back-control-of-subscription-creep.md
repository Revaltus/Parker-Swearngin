## LinkedIn

A $29 charge never gets anyone's attention. Forty of them do.

In the example audit from our latest post, a ten-person HVAC subcontractor was paying $586 a month in recurring charges: fleet GPS for a truck that had been sold, 12 project management seats with only 7 active users, two overlapping cloud storage plans, and an old scheduling app. The total was $7,032 a year. One afternoon of work recovered about $2,900, roughly 41 percent.

This matters most for KC service businesses with lumpy income. Progress billing and slow-paying GCs can leave cash arriving 45 to 60 days after the work is done, but subscriptions still draw on the 1st, the 8th, and the 22nd. Deductible does not mean free, either. At a 30 percent combined rate, $1,000 of unused tools still costs you $700 in cash.

The post walks through a seven-step audit built on three months of statements, then shows how to give subscriptions their own budget line with a ten-minute monthly review.
