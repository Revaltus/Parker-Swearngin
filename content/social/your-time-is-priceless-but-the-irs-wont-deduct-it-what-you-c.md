## LinkedIn

A drywall or concrete subcontractor drives a three-quarter-ton truck to a Lee's Summit food pantry 30 times a year. That is 660 miles. At the 14-cent charitable rate, the deduction is $92.40. With actual gas at $3.30 a gallon, it is $181.50.

Same volunteering, nearly double the deduction. The catch is a log showing the date, destination, purpose, and miles for every trip.

This is the pattern with KC service business owners. The IRS will not deduct the value of your volunteer hours, so forty hours at an $85 rate is $3,400 that never shows up on the return. But unreimbursed mileage, parking, tolls, supplies, and required uniforms for a qualified charity can count if you itemize. Once you reach $250 for a single charity, you also need a written acknowledgment in hand before you file.

Our latest post covers what qualifies, what does not (child care, lost billable time), and a simple phone-note system for tracking it as you go.
