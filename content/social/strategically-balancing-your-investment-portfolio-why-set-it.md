## LinkedIn

A $1,000,000 account set at 60/40 becomes 68/32 after a 40 percent stock rally, without a single trade.

That is the quiet problem for HVAC, drywall, concrete, and asphalt owners in KC. The brokerage account gets opened in a good year. Then a GC stretches payment to 75 days, the crew grows, and nobody looks at the allocation again. The extra stock risk shows up in the next downturn, right when you need cash for payroll and sub payments.

Selling everything is rarely the answer. In our example, getting back to 60/40 means selling about $96,000 of stock, which could cost roughly $7,200 in federal tax in a taxable account. So the order matters: fund an operating reserve, fund a tax reserve, then rebalance the long-term bucket with new contributions, trades inside a 401(k) or IRA, or loss harvesting.

Our CPA-led team looks at your entity, retirement plan, and cash flow together, so the rebalance does not create a surprise tax bill.
