## What is a cost segregation study for an investment property?

Buy a rental, an office, or a commercial building, and the tax code normally has you deduct its cost slowly, over decades. A cost segregation study speeds that up. It breaks the property into components, such as certain flooring, fixtures, and site improvements, that qualify for much shorter depreciation lives. More of the deduction lands in the early years, which lowers your current taxes and keeps cash in your hands.

This matters most when your business is growing. Brokers who buy investment property, move into an office they own, or add rental holdings alongside their brokerage are the ones who tend to benefit. Results depend on the property and your overall tax picture, and we never promise a specific savings number. What we can do is tell you honestly whether a study is worth the cost.

Call us before closing or right after it. Timing affects how the deduction fits into your year, and it is far easier to plan than to repair. Because the firm handles your year-round tax planning and your tax preparation, the study feeds directly into your return and your quarterly estimates.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Real estate CPA services

**Q: What does a CPA do for a real estate broker?**
A: A CPA for real estate brokers prepares tax returns, plans quarterly estimates around closing dates, tracks deductible expenses, and advises on entity structure and investment property purchases. Our Lee's Summit team also keeps your books and payroll current, so you see your cash position and tax exposure before a bill arrives.

**Q: How are realtor quarterly tax payments calculated in Missouri?**
A: Estimates should rest on your projected income for the year, covering federal and Missouri tax, not a flat quarterly split. Because commissions are uneven, we tie payments to actual closings and your pending pipeline, then adjust after big closings. That helps you avoid underpayment penalties without overpaying early.

**Q: What is a cost segregation study and who benefits?**
A: A cost segregation study reviews an investment property and separates components, such as certain fixtures and site improvements, that depreciate faster than the building itself. That can move deductions into the early years. Brokers who buy rental, office, or commercial property benefit most, though results vary by property and tax situation.

**Q: Should a real estate agent be an S-corp or a sole proprietor?**
A: It depends on net income and how stable it is. An S-corp can reduce self-employment tax once profit comfortably supports a reasonable salary, but it adds payroll, a separate tax return, and administrative cost. We model both structures with your actual numbers before recommending a change.

**Q: Can you handle payroll and 1099s for my brokerage?**
A: Yes. We process payroll, filings, and W2s for your employees, and 1099s for the independent contractor agents your brokerage pays. Everything ties back to your books, so contractor payments, commission splits, and payroll taxes agree with each other at year-end instead of requiring cleanup.

**Q: How does the fixed monthly fee work?**
A: Tax, accounting, payroll, and advisory services are bundled into one fixed monthly fee, agreed on before work begins. You aren't billed hourly for a question or a busy closing season, so costs stay predictable even though your income doesn't. We'll outline exactly what's included during your consultation.
