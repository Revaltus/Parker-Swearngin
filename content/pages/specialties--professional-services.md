## Owner pay and entity structure as your firm adds principals

Three things tend to send a principal looking for a new accountant: the firm adds a partner, a new principal comes in, or the tax bill is bigger than anyone expected. All three lead to the same two questions. What is reasonable pay for the owners, and does the current entity still fit the firm?

We review owner compensation against what the work and the numbers support. Pay set too low invites IRS attention, and pay set too high gives away tax savings. Then we look at the structure itself. A sole proprietorship, LLC, S corporation, or partnership can each make sense at a different stage, and the right answer often changes when ownership does.

That review is our Entity Type Analysis. We model the options with your real numbers, explain the trade-offs in plain language, and give you a recommendation before the decision is due. For firms looking into entity type analysis for professional services, the goal is simple: keep more of what the principals earn, and have a clear reason for how the firm is set up. If a partner buy-in or a new principal is on the horizon, call us early.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Accountant for professional-service firms

**Q: What does an accountant for an engineering or architecture firm do beyond taxes?**
A: Beyond the return, the work is books that show profit by project, payroll with W2 and 1099 processing, year-round tax planning, and advisory work like Entity Type Analysis and Retirement Plan Analysis. **Q: Are R&D tax credits available to engineering and architecture firms?**
A: Some engineering and design work can qualify for R&D credits, but eligibility depends on the specific projects and the documentation behind them. We don't promise a credit. **Q: How does the fixed monthly fee work?**
